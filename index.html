A day at Little Star

A gentle rhythm, from first hug to last wave.

Little ones thrive on rhythm. Our schedule is predictable enough to feel safe and flexible enough to follow a child's curiosity. Tap any moment to peek inside it.

  1. Slow mornings on purpose. Quiet free play, cuddles for sleepy arrivals, and a calm handoff so goodbyes stay short and sweet. I'll text you a "settled in" photo if drop-off was teary.

  2. We eat together at one small table — pouring, passing, and "please" practice included. Menus are posted weekly and allergies are taken seriously.

  3. Rain boots welcome. Our fenced backyard has a garden the children help tend, a mud kitchen, a water table, and room to run. We go out in nearly every weather — dressed for it.

  4. Songs, stories, and our play-based Little Learners curriculum: letters and numbers hiding inside games, fine-motor work at the art shelf, and big questions from small people.

  5. Real food from my kitchen — think veggie pasta, rice bowls, soup with warm bread. Picky eaters get patience, not pressure, and you'll see what was eaten in your Daily Star Report.

  6. Each child has their own cot and bedding in our dim, cozy nap room — soft music, a star nightlight, and back-rubs for the ones who need them. Non-nappers rest with quiet books.

  7. Paint, playdough, sensory bins, and gloriously messy process art. It's about the doing, not the fridge-worthy result — though you'll get plenty of those too.

  8. Back outside or deep into block cities and dress-up. Mixed ages play together here — the big kids mentor, the little ones stretch, and everyone belongs.

  9. You get a real handoff, not a hallway wave — the day's highlight, anything to watch for, and a child who usually asks for "five more minutes." Doors close at [Hours e.g. 7:00 AM–5:30 PM].

Why families choose us

Six promises, in writing.

Every worry parents have about home daycare — we've heard it, lived it, and built a named promise to answer it. These aren't slogans; they're in our parent handbook.

The Backup Care Promise

"What if she's sick or on vacation?"

Our full closure calendar is published a year ahead, every January. For the unexpected, we keep a vetted, background-checked substitute and a partner provider on call — and you'll hear from me by 6:00 AM, never at the door.

Daily Star Reports

"Will I know how her day went?"

Photos, meals, naps, diapers, and milestones — delivered to your phone through our parent app every single day. Bumps and tears are reported the moment they happen, not at pickup.

Open-Door Policy

"What happens when I'm not there?"

Drop in any time, unannounced — nursing moms especially welcome. Secure coded entry, interior cameras, and every adult in this household fingerprinted and background-checked. Nothing here is behind a curtain.

Screen-Free Days

"Is it just TV and babysitting?"

There is no television in our playroom and no tablet in our day. Children here are read to, played with, and talked to — by an adult on the floor with them, not on a phone.

Little Learners Curriculum

"Will he be ready for kindergarten?"

Play-based doesn't mean plan-free. Weekly themes build early literacy, numbers, and social skills — and our graduates walk into kindergarten confident, curious, and already in love with books.

Small by Design

"Will she get lost in the crowd?"

Four children, maximum. Ever. A 1:4 ratio means your child's cues are caught early, friendships go deep, and separation anxiety fades fast — because the same arms greet them every morning.

Questions parents actually ask

Ask us anything.

What happens if you're sick or take a vacation?

This is the number-one fear about home daycare, so it's our number-one promise. All planned closures are published a full year ahead every January — you'll never be surprised by a vacation. For true emergencies, a vetted, background-checked substitute your child has already met steps in, and I have a reciprocal backup arrangement with a licensed provider nearby. You'll hear from me by 6:00 AM at the latest, with a plan, not just a problem.

Will I get updates during the day?

Every single day. Your Daily Star Report arrives through our parent app with photos, meals, nap times, diapers/potty, and a note about what your child worked on or loved. Anything out of the ordinary — a bump, a hard moment, a low appetite — you hear about it when it happens, not at pickup.

Can I visit before enrolling? Can I drop in after?

Yes, and yes — unannounced, any time your child is in care. Our Open-Door Policy is exactly what it sounds like. A program that discourages visits is waving a red flag; we'd rather you see an ordinary Tuesday than a polished tour.

How much screen time do the children get?

None. There's no TV in the playroom and no tablets in our day. Little Star days are built from play, books, art, real conversation, and outdoor time. (On rare special occasions — a snowed-in movie morning maybe twice a year — parents are told in advance.)

What's your illness policy?

Children need to be fever-free, vomit-free, and symptom-free for 24 hours without medication before returning. It protects every child here — including yours on the weeks they're healthy. The full policy, including the specific symptom list, is in the Parent Handbook you'll get before you tour.

How many children are in your care? Who else is in the home?

Four children maximum — a 1:4 ratio, intentionally below what our license allows. You'll know every member of my household by name, and every adult living here is fingerprinted and background-checked through the state. Nobody unvetted is ever around the children, period.

What does a typical day look like?

A gentle, predictable rhythm: free play from 7:00, family-style breakfast, outdoor time every day, circle and Little Learners activities, a home-cooked lunch, a real nap in a cozy nap room, art and sensory play, and golden-hour outside time before pickup. Scroll up to "A Day at Little Star" for the full timeline.

How do I know a home daycare is safe and legitimate?

Ask for three things anywhere you tour: the license (ours is #[License #], displayed by the door and verifiable with the state), the latest licensing inspection report (we'll hand you ours), and proof of CPR/First Aid certification and insurance. If a provider hesitates on any of these — keep looking. Our free parent guide above walks you through all ten questions worth asking.
